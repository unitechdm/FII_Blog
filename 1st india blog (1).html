UNITECH Engineering Story Series — 20 Years, 2006–2026

What if the machine you rely on today didn't exist yesterday?

Someone had to build it first.

Every screen printing business owner has stood in front of a machine and thought: this is just how it's done. The auto-take system that pulls prints off the belt. The diagnostic panel that tells you what's wrong before you call a technician. The frame that stretches a screen twice the size anyone said was possible.

None of that was inevitable. Every one of those machines exists because, at some point, a customer had a problem no catalogue could solve — and someone had to build the answer from scratch. This is the story of what that actually looks like, told through eight moments over twenty years.

2006 · Ecomatic

When automation felt out of reach

For a long time, automation in screen printing meant one thing: expensive. Small and mid-sized print shops watched larger competitors automate while they stayed dependent on manual labor — not because they didn't want consistency, but because the cost of getting it felt out of reach.

The real question wasn't "how do we build a better machine." It was: how do we make automation something more businesses can actually afford? That shift — from automation as a luxury to automation as something accessible — became Ecomatic, India's first economical automation platform, built to handle everything from paper to metal to plastic in one frame.

2007 · Self Diagnostic System

When the machine goes quiet and no one knows why

Automation solves one problem and creates another: when something stops working, most shops are stuck guessing. A machine halts mid-run, and the operator's only tools are trial, error, and a phone call to service.

The problem underneath that is simple — not knowing is what actually costs time. So instead of just fixing breakdowns faster, the question became: why can't the machine tell you what's wrong itself? That became SDS — turning "unknown" into information, and cutting the gap between a machine stopping and a problem getting solved.

2008 · Mega Machines, 166×166 → 332×332

When the machine you need doesn't exist yet

Sometimes the issue isn't quality or downtime — it's that your requirement simply isn't on the market. A customer needing a 166×166 format searched across India and found nothing built for that scale. Standard machines topped out well below what the job required.

That's a different kind of problem: not "how do we improve this," but "does this even exist?" The answer was to build it — and then, two years later, to go bigger again, engineering a 332×332 frame that held size and tension under control at a scale no standard machine had attempted. Both exist today because someone said "let's take the challenge" instead of "that's not available."

2009 · Auto Take System

When the technology is ready before the market is

Not every good idea gets adopted immediately. The first automatic take-off system launched in 2009, promising less manual handling and fewer rejected prints from inconsistent pulls. The technology worked. Adoption still took a decade.

That's a real problem too, and an easy one to get wrong: assuming a slow start means a bad idea. What it actually meant was that the industry's mindset needed time to catch up — until automation went from "optional extra" to "necessary."

2013 · Optical Electronics

When your industry starts changing shape

By 2013, screen printing itself was evolving — some customers weren't printing designs anymore, they were printing circuits. Mesh and frames were being asked to lay down PCB traces and diode components for a new field: printed electronics.

That's a problem of direction, not defects — figuring out where the industry is actually headed and whether your equipment can go there with it. Reaching into research labs and institutes to support that shift meant screen printing stopped being just a print process and started being a manufacturing platform for electronics.

2018 · UniWash

When "good enough" mesh cleaning isn't good enough

For screen washing, the recurring complaints were consistent: too much manual labor, too much water wasted, and mesh quality that varied from wash to wash. None of those are exotic problems — they're the kind that quietly erode margins every single day.

Solving all three at once, without trading one for another, became UniWash — a fully automatic, enclosed washing system built around one customer's specific need for water savings, which then became a standard offering because the problem turned out to be universal.

2022 · UniStretch, worldwide patent

When one solution needs to fit every size

The last piece was flexibility itself. A stretching technology that only works for one frame size isn't really solving the underlying problem — which is that print businesses grow, shrink, and diversify, and their equipment needs to grow with them.

UniStretch, now under worldwide patent, was built around that idea directly: one core system, engineered to scale across small, medium, large, and custom mega frames — so the technology doesn't get outgrown.

As long as the dream grows, the machine can grow with it. — UNITECH, on UniStretch

The pattern underneath all of it

None of these machines started as a spec sheet. Every one started as a specific business hitting a specific wall — cost, downtime, missing capacity, wasted water, an industry shifting under their feet — and a decision to solve it rather than wait for someone else to.

That's really the lesson in twenty years of milestones: the machine on your floor today is the result of a problem someone refused to leave unsolved. The next machine your business needs might not exist yet either.
